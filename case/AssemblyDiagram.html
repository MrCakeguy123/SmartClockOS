SmartClockOS wall case v2 — how it goes together

No screws anywhere in the assembly. The only screws are the two that go in the wall. Arrow colors: red = display board, orange = back cover, green = BME280 sensor & wire, blue = wall mounting.

1 · Exploded view — the whole stack

  1. RED: lay the shell face-down and lower the display board straight in, screen toward the window. It should drop in with zero force and sit on the four round corner pads. If it doesn't fall in freely, something (a wire) is under it — don't press.
  2. GREEN: plug the sensor wires into the top JST, run the bundle down the back of the board and through the round hole into the chin pod, then seat the BME280 inside the pod (sensor can facing the front slits).
  3. ORANGE — the part that changed: hold the back cover tilted about 15°, top edge first. Slide the top edge under the two lips just inside the shell's top wall, then swing the bottom edge down and press until you hear two clicks — the two hooks at the bottom edge locking into their slots. That's it.
Opening it later: push the two hooks inward through the slots in the bottom wall (fingernail or small screwdriver, they sit about 4 mm deep), swing the bottom edge of the cover out, then unhook the top edge. Never pry the middle of the cover.

2 · Sensor wire route

Top JST → over the back of the board → down through the pod hole → sensor sits in the chin, away from the warm electronics so it reads true room temperature. Leave a little slack at the JST end so the plug isn't strained.

3 · Hanging it on the wall

  1. Two screws in the wall, 100 mm apart, dead level (use the shell as a drill template if you like). Any #6 / 3.5 mm screw with a head up to 7.5 mm works.
  2. Leave the heads proud: ~5 mm normally, ~8 mm if a fan is installed (the corner feet need a bigger air gap to feed the intake grille).
  3. BLUE: hang the big keyhole openings over the heads and slide the case down until it locks. To take it off: lift, then pull.

Print settings (PLA)

Nozzle 205–215 °C · bed 60 °C · 0.2 mm layers · 4 perimeters (this matters — the hooks and lips are solid at 4 walls) · 15–25% infill · no supports for either part · shell prints face-down, lid STL is already flipped the right way. PLA is fine for a wall clock; just keep it out of direct summer-car-window sun.
